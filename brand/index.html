    Why it matters

    Across Manila, “28-day aged” is already a menu line. Written as numerals, our name would read as somebody else’s claim. Written as XXVIII, it reads as a mark, and as ours.

    The Roman numerals also slow the eye down. You read XXVIII the way you read a date on a cornerstone: carefully.

    Values & voice

    What we believe, and how it sounds out loud.

      The tone

      • Calm.We never raise our voice, in print or in person.
      • Exact.Days, grams, times. Numbers over adjectives.
      • Warm.The beef is the reason. The guest is the point.
      • Unhurried.Short sentences, room to breathe, no countdowns.

      Calls to action

      Every invitation leads to the same place, a table. Each one is written for the moment it sits in, so no two buttons on a page say the same thing.

      • One goal.A held table, or a private-room enquiry. Nothing else gets a solid brass button.
      • One line per moment.Name what the guest is looking at: the cut, the room, the drink. Never repeat a line on the same page.
      • No pressure.No “now”, no “hurry”, no countdowns. The Long Wait is the only thing we ask guests to order early.
      • Verb first, short.Two to six words, sentence case in the copy, set in Big Shoulders capitals on the button.

      Clear space

      Measure x: the gap the diamond leaves in the rule under the mark. Keep 2x clear on every side. Nothing enters that zone: not type, not an edge, not a photograph.

      On photographs

      Only where the picture is quiet: the bar, the booths, the dark above the plate. Never on the food.

      On screen: brass foil

      The mark is never flat yellow on a screen. It is struck in foil, like the menu cover: a darker brass at the edges, one bright band, a light top edge and a shadow beneath.

      • The gradient#8A6A3A → #C29A5B → #F4DFB4 → #C29A5B → #8A6A3A, at 100°.
      • The lightOn a computer the bright band follows the pointer. On a phone it sweeps once every seven seconds. With reduced motion it stands still.
      • WhereThe logo and wordmark only: header, phone menu, footer, intro, menu cover. Never on body type, buttons or prices.
      • PrintThe same mark becomes real brass foil. The PDFs carry a separate foil plate.

        The library

              XI

              Motion

              Twelve slow camera moves. Eight seconds each, silent, on a loop.

              1. Always muted.No soundtrack, no voice-over. The room is quiet.
              2. Eight-second loops.Use the whole move. Never trim it short.
              3. Slow moves only.Glide, tilt, dolly, arc, orbit, crane. No whip pans, no zoom punches.
              4. No cut under 2 s.In an edit, every shot holds for at least two seconds.
              5. Type in the empty side.Words sit where the frame is quiet, never over the plate.

              On every page: preload="none", two videos playing at most, SD files on phones and data saver, a still frame under reduced motion. Hover or focus a tile to play it; press it to open the player and check the social crops.

                        XV

                        Menu system

                        The courses renamed for the room they come from.

                        Was, now

                          Setting a menu

                          • Dish namesGloock, 22–26 pt, sentence case as written in the data.
                          • DescriptionsHanken Grotesk, 10–11 pt. Ingredients, not adjectives. One line.
                          • PricesBig Shoulders 600, right-aligned, no decimals, peso sign.
                          • Age and weightEvery steak lists its age in the ageing tag. Weight beside it.
                          • SectionsOpen with the diamond rule and one short line.
                          • Photos leadEvery dish is pictured. The photograph comes first and type never sits on the food.
                          • AllergensOne line at the foot of every menu page, Hanken 9 pt: “Tell us about any allergy. The kitchen will talk you through every dish.”
